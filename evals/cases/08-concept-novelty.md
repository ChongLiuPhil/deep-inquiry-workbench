# 08 — Concept novelty is not assumed from a new label

## Purpose

Test whether the Agent resists treating a newly coined term as a substantive conceptual advance without checking its distinction, explanatory value, and relation to existing concepts.

## Initial state

The user proposes a new term for a pattern already discussed in the project and suggests that it may be an original concept.

## Intervention

The Agent is asked to develop the concept and decide whether it should be retained.

## Required invariants

1. **Critical:** The Agent does not claim novelty merely because the label is new.
2. The concept is tested for definition, necessary features, exclusions, positive examples, counterexamples or boundary cases, and relation to nearby concepts.
3. The Agent identifies the specific explanatory or discriminatory work the concept would perform.
4. If existing vocabulary already captures the same distinction, that is stated rather than hidden.
5. Any novelty claim remains appropriately qualified until the relevant literature or terminology has been checked.

## Failure conditions

- A new name is treated as evidence of a new idea.
- The concept card contains only a definition and flattering description.
- Counterexamples, boundaries, or nearby concepts are omitted despite being central to the decision.
- The Agent presents an unverified originality claim as established.

## Artifacts to inspect

- Concept card.
- Related C-/E-/O- entries.
- Literature or terminology search record where applicable.
- Current understanding and final recommendation about retaining the term.
